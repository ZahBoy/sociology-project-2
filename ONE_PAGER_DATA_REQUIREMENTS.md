### The Methodological Dilemma: The Observational Equivalence of Cross-Sectional PISA Data
In standard cross-sectional surveys like PISA or NMT administrative registries, we observe a robust empirical correlation: higher socio-economic status (SES) and metropolitan school location predict superior test scores ($\beta_{SES} > 0$). However, this cross-sectional snapshot suffers from **observational equivalence**: all three major sociological paradigms (Structural Functionalism, Bourdieusian Cultural Reproduction, and Rational Action Theory) predict this exact same statistical gradient, but attribute it to mutually incompatible causal mechanisms. 

To break this empirical deadlock and test which paradigm truly explains the Ukrainian testing puzzle, sociologists require specialized, multi-level, and longitudinal data architectures that measure the hidden causal gears within families, schools, and markets.

---

### 1. Data Needed to Test Structural Functionalism (Meritocratic Human Capital)
*Core Claim to Verify: Test scores reflect genuine, acquired cognitive competence and individual effort allocated meritocratically to functional roles.*

To test this mechanism, we need data that isolates **innate/prior cognitive ability** and **individual learning investments** from parental background:
1. **Pre-School Cognitive Baseline Measures:** Standardized early-childhood cognitive assessments (administered at ages 3-5, prior to extensive formal socialization) to control for baseline intelligence and track value-added learning.
2. **High-Resolution Time-Use Diaries:** Detailed daily logging of actual academic study hours, focused homework time, and self-directed problem solving versus leisure, capturing individual effort and perseverance.
3. **Objective School Input Indicators:** Micro-data on teacher pedagogical qualifications, per-pupil lab equipment, and curriculum fidelity, testing whether school-level resource provision directly translates into measurable human capital gains.
4. **Longitudinal Occupational Returns:** 10-year tracer panel data linking student PISA/NMT scores to subsequent labor market productivity, wage premiums, and job complexity, verifying whether the testing system actually sorts talent efficiently according to societal functional needs.

---

### 2. Data Needed to Test Bourdieu's Cultural Reproduction (Habitus & Symbolic Violence)
*Core Claim to Verify: Standardized tests are culturally biased instruments that misrecognize class-based habitus and linguistic familiarity as natural academic merit.*

To test this mechanism, we require qualitative, ethnographic, and psychometric indicators capable of measuring the **arbitrary cultural codes** of the educational institution:
1. **Classroom Linguistic & Interactional Ethnography:** Audio/video recordings of classroom discourse and oral examinations, analyzed via computational linguistics to measure whether teachers reward elite linguistic registers, self-assured bodily postures (*habitus*), and cultural references over raw technical accuracy.
2. **Item-Level Differential Item Functioning (DIF):** Psychometric Item Response Theory (IRT) analyses of individual PISA / NMT test items to identify DIF: testing whether students from rural backgrounds with the *same latent math ability* systematically fail text-heavy or culturally contextualized questions compared to urban peers.
3. **Teacher Stereotyping & Double-Blind Experimental Testing:** Factorial survey experiments (vignette studies) presenting identical student essays to Ukrainian teachers while randomly altering student socio-economic profiles (e.g., child of a village farmer vs. child of a Kyiv lawyer) to quantify implicit bias and labeling effects.
4. **Subjective Feelings of Alienation / Symbolic Violence:** Survey scales measuring student "sense of academic illegitimacy" (imposter syndrome, feeling that the school language is "alien" or "not for people like me") to test Bourdieu's concept of *méconnaissance*.

---

### 3. Data Needed to Test Rational Action Theory (Relative Risk Aversion & Shadow Education)
*Core Claim to Verify: Disparities stem from rational, forward-looking household decisions where high-SES families invest heavily in test preparation to prevent downward social mobility.*

To test this mechanism, we require financial micro-data and subjective decision-making metrics modeled after Boudon and Breen-Goldthorpe:
1. **Disaggregated Shadow Education Household Expenditures:** Exact financial tracking of household spending on private NMT tutors, commercial online prep courses, and admissions consultants (measured as a percentage of total family disposable income).
2. **Subjective Risk and Probability Elicitation:** Quantitative measurement of subjective probabilities: (a) perceived likelihood of passing the NMT, (b) perceived risk of downward social mobility if the child does not obtain a university degree, and (c) subjective assessment of minimum acceptable social status.
3. **Multi-Stage Branching Point Tracking (Boudon Decomposition):** Panel data tracking students across the Grade 9 institutional branching point (academic lyceum vs. vocational college), testing whether high-SES students choose academic tracks even when their prior academic performance is mediocre (isolating the secondary effect of stratification).
4. **Direct Cost & Opportunity Cost Metrics:** Regional data on the financial costs of living away from home (dormitory fees, living expenses in Kyiv/Lviv) versus local vocational earnings, modeling the rational choice boundary for rural families.

---

### 4. Essential Structural Controls: Ukrainian Wartime Disruption Metrics
In the context of Ukraine (PISA 2022 and 2025), any valid sociological test must disentangle theoretical mechanisms from the severe structural shocks of war:
1. **Direct Conflict Disruption Metrics:** Cumulative hours spent in bomb shelters during school hours, frequency and duration of power outages (blackouts), and physical facility damage.
2. **Modality of Instruction:** Continuous indicators of fully offline, hybrid, versus asynchronous remote learning.
3. **Displacement & Separation Status:** Disaggregating internally displaced persons (IDP), returnees, and students whose families are separated by active military service.


---

### Summary: The Ideal Ukrainian Educational Panel Study (UEPS)
The definitive test of sociological paradigms in Ukraine requires an integrated **Ukrainian Educational Panel Study (UEPS)** that follows a cohort of students from age 10 to 25. By combining **pre-school cognitive baselines (Functionalism)**, **linguistic ethnography of classroom habitus (Bourdieu)**, and **household shadow-education financial ledgers with subjective risk matrices (Rational Action)**, Ukrainian sociologists could finally isolate the true mechanisms driving educational inequality and design evidence-based policies for a more equitable post-war educational reconstruction.
